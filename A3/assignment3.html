Results¶

PCA was fitted to the four flower features from the data: sepal length, sepal width, petal length, and petal width. The features were centred automatically by scikit-learn's PCA function before the principal-component directions were calculated.

The vectors describing the first three PCA axes are:

$$ \mathbf{w}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3614 \\ -0.0845 \\ 0.8567 \\ 0.3583 \end{bmatrix} $$

$$ \mathbf{w}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6566 \\ 0.7302 \\ -0.1734 \\ -0.0755 \end{bmatrix} $$

$$ \mathbf{w}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} -0.5820 \\ 0.5979 \\ 0.0762 \\ 0.5458 \end{bmatrix} $$

Each coefficient describes the contribution of an original feature to a principal component axis. PC1 is influenced most strongly by petal length, while PC2 is influenced mainly by sepal length and sepal width. PC3 contrasts sepal length with sepal width and petal width.

The 1D projection onto PC1 clearly separates Setosa from the other two species, although some overlap remains between Versicolor and Virginica. The 2D projection onto PC1 and PC2 shows three visible groups. Setosa is clearly separated, while Versicolor and Virginica are mostly separated with some overlap. Adding PC3 provides another viewing direction, but it does not produce a major visual improvement over the first two principal components.

variance_ratios = pca_full.explained_variance_ratio_
# running total of explained variances
cumulative_variance = np.cumsum(variance_ratios)
# display variance table 
variance_table = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        "Explained variance (%)": variance_ratios * 100,
        "Cumulative variance (%)": cumulative_variance * 100
    },
    index=["PC1", "PC2", "PC3", "PC4"]
)
display(variance_table.round(2))
# display output for q1 
variance_explained_2d = cumulative_variance[1]
print("Variance explained by PC1 and PC2:",f"{variance_explained_2d:.2%}")

# check q1 variance output with Frobenius norm
# centre the original data using PCA mean 
X_centered = X - pca_full.mean_
# total variance = sq Frobenius norm of centered data 
total_variation = (np.linalg.norm(X_centered.to_numpy(), ord="fro") ** 2)
# 2d varaince = sq Frobenius norm of the first two PCA coordinates
captured_variation_2d = (np.linalg.norm(X_pca_full[:, :2], ord="fro") ** 2)

Q1:¶

PC1 explains approximately 92.46% of the total variance, and PC2 explains approximately 5.31%. Therefore, the two-dimensional representation using PC1 and PC2 explains approximately 97.77% of the total variance.

This result was verified using the relationship between variance and the squared Frobenius norm:

$$ \frac{\left\|Z_{\mathrm{2D}}\right\|_F^2} {\left\|X_{\mathrm{centered}}\right\|_F^2} \approx 0.9777 $$

where the Frobenius-norm calculation agrees with the cumulative explained variance produced by PCA.

Results¶

ISOMAP was used to reduce the four-dimensional Iris dataset to two dimensions using three nearest neighbours. Unlike PCA, which finds linear directions of maximum variance, ISOMAP constructs a neighbourhood graph and uses shortest-path distances through the graph to approximate nonlinear geodesic distances.

Both the PCA and ISOMAP visualizations clearly separate Setosa from the other two species. In the PCA representation, Versicolor and Virginica form mostly separate groups, although there is some overlap between them. ISOMAP produces a more curved nonlinear arrangement of the data, but Versicolor and Virginica still overlap. Therefore, the nonlinear representation does not provide a clear visual improvement over PCA for this dataset.

To compare the classification ability of the representations, a five-nearest-neighbours classifier was evaluated using five-fold stratified cross-validation. The original four-dimensional data produced a mean accuracy of 96.67%, with a standard deviation of 2.98 percentage points. The two-dimensional PCA representation also produced a mean accuracy of 96.67%, with a smaller standard deviation of 2.11 percentage points. The two-dimensional ISOMAP representation produced a lower mean accuracy of 92.00%, with a larger standard deviation of 5.42 percentage points.

These results indicate that PCA is preferable for this dataset. PCA reduces the data from four dimensions to two without reducing the mean classification accuracy, and its accuracy is more consistent across the cross-validation folds. From Part 4, the two PCA components also retain approximately 97.77% of the total variance.

In comparison, ISOMAP has lower and less consistent classification accuracy. With three nearest neighbours, the ISOMAP implementation adds connections between the separate groups before calculating the embedding, indicating that the ISOMAP result is sensitive to the selected number of neighbours.

Overall, PCA provides the better two-dimensional representation of the Iris dataset because it preserves most of the variance, maintains the classification accuracy of the original data, and produces class separation that is at least as clear as the nonlinear ISOMAP representation.
